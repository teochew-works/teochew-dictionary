# ADR-0027: Publish a Resynthesised Speaker Tier Derived from the Project's Own Recordings

## Status

✅ Accepted

## Context

[ADR-0016](adr-0016.md) rejected synthesis as a way to *fill* the audio gap, and said in its own
Consequences that "the decision rests on the state of the tooling, not on a principle against
synthesis". Two things have changed since (issue #259).

First, the gap it was weighed against is gone: every attested Chaozhou syllable — 3,088 of them —
has a recording by one speaker (issue #36, closed 2026-09-06). The corpus problem is no longer
coverage but **consistency**. Those clips were recorded across many sessions, and `npm run
audio:grade` measures how much they vary in dimensions the tones themselves do not: level spans
11.7 dB between the 5th and 95th percentile; each clip carries ~400 ms of dead air, and no two the
same amount; the voiced span within one tone varies by ±80 ms; the pitch register within one tone
by ±10–25 Hz. A learner drilling syllables back to back hears those jumps as noise between the
things they are meant to compare.

Second, the tooling ADR-0016 surveyed — TTS voices, an eSpeak-NG profile — is not what is now
proposed. A WORLD-vocoder *resynthesis* of the speaker's own clip keeps that clip's spectral
envelope and aperiodicity (the speaker, the segments, the initial→vowel coarticulation) and
replaces only the between-take variation: the f0 contour, the onset and voiced durations, the
level, the silence around it. The targets it renders toward are not this project's Chao-numeral
analysis but the **speaker's own per-tone medians** measured from the same corpus
(`src/audio/grade.ts`, filed per tone, per tone × coda class, per initial), and every render is
measured again by the same extractor and rejected if it lands more than 1.5 robust σ from what it
set out to do (`checkRender` in `src/audio/targets.ts`). On the current corpus 3,079 of 3,087
renders pass. The failure mode of ADR-0016's TTS — fluent, confidently wrong Teochew — is structurally
unavailable to it: nothing is generated that the speaker did not say.

What it *can* do is what ADR-0016's second Consequence warned of: present a processed clip as if
it were a recording. That is the part this record has to answer.

## Decision

We will publish resynthesised clips as **a distinct speaker tier, additive to and never replacing
the recordings they derive from.** Concretely:

- A rendered clip is published under its own speaker id, `<speaker>-n` (`jky-n` for the current
  corpus), as a second `clips.<syllable>[]` entry in `data/phonology/audio/<variety>.yaml`. The
  original clip, its `checksum`, and its `speaker` are never modified.
- A rendered clip carries its provenance in the manifest: `synthesis: world-retune` and
  `derivedFrom: sha256:<checksum of the source clip>` (both fields on `audioClip` in
  `packages/core/src/schema/phonology.ts`, required together), and cites the new `kind: import`
  source id `teochew-dictionary-audio-resynth` in `data/sources.yaml`, whose licence is that of
  the source recordings (CC-BY-4.0) because a render is a derivative of them.
- Its `confidence` is capped at `medium` by a schema refine. `selectPrimaryClip`
  (`src/build/enrich.ts`) therefore keeps the human recording as default playback; the tier is
  reachable through the existing per-speaker buttons. Promoting it to default is a separate
  decision, taken per corpus after listening through, by raising that one field.
- The web UI labels it: `AudioReference` and `SoundClip` carry `synthesis`, and both clip buttons
  render a badge and an `aria-label` suffix when it is set, so a learner cannot mistake the tier
  for a recording — on the Sounds tab in particular, where a clip's button *is* its speaker id.
- Only renders that pass `audio:synthesize`'s self-check are published, and publishing stays a
  human CLI step (`merge:resynth`), dry-run by default, in keeping with ADR-0017.
- Render output lives in `.cache/audio-synth/` (ADR-0013), not `data/staging/`: unlike a
  recording it is reproducible from the cached clips and the code.

We will **still not** synthesise a syllable that has no recording, nor another variety's
pronunciation from this speaker's clips. ADR-0016's rejection of *generating* Teochew stands; this
record narrows it to what it was actually about.

**Rejected: normalising the recordings in place.** Re-encoding and re-uploading each clip under
its existing entry would need no schema change, but it destroys the independent evidence ADR-0016
values, is irreversible once the old Release assets are reclaimed (issue #241), and leaves nothing
to compare a render against. **Rejected: metadata-only consistency** — running the silence-trim
backfill (issue #252) and adding a per-clip gain applied through `HTMLAudioElement.volume`. It
would fix the two largest measured inconsistencies with no new bytes, and remains a reasonable
first step for a corpus without this pipeline; but `volume` can only attenuate (ADR-0026's CORS
wall rules out Web Audio gain), and it cannot touch pitch or duration at all. **Rejected: a third
`SOURCE_KIND`** (`synthesized`). `kind` answers whether copying a source creates a licence
obligation (ADR-0009), not how a clip was produced; a render's obligation is exactly its source
recording's, so provenance belongs on the clip, and the licence machinery needs no new branch.

## Consequences

- Positive: a learner can opt into a corpus where every syllable sits at the same level, in the
  same register for its tone, with the same silence around it — and the tone contours they hear
  are the speaker's own typical production, not an idealisation.
- Positive: reversible. The tier is one `speaker` id and one `sources` id; deleting those entries
  restores ADR-0016's world exactly, and the bytes can be re-rendered from the recordings at any
  time.
- **ADR-0016's "every clip in the dataset is a human recording" is no longer true**, and that
  record is amended to say so. What remains true, and is now enforced by schema rather than
  assumed: every clip is either a human recording or is labelled, provenance-linked, and
  confidence-capped as derived from one.
- Negative: two `packages/core` schema fields and an `AudioReference` field are an exported-type
  change — a minor version bump (`0.4.0`) and a tarball release before the mobile app can read the
  new manifest (ADR-0024).
- Negative: a second clip per syllable roughly doubles the asset count. GitHub's 1000-assets-per-
  release cap (ADR-0014, issue #228) means the tier is spread across its own `audio-<variety>-
  resynth-N` releases, and `audio:verify` runs take twice as long.
- Neutral: the "circular evidence" concern of ADR-0016 is addressed by construction, not by policy —
  the targets are measured from the recordings, the recordings stay published as the primary
  evidence, and the derived tier says what it is.

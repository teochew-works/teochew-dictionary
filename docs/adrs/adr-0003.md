# ADR-0003: Confidence and Sources on Every Phonology Mapping

## Status

✅ Accepted

## Context

Because IPA is derived rather than stored (ADR-0001), the phonology tables carry the whole weight
of the dataset's phonetic claims. Those claims are not uniformly solid. Published descriptions of
Teochew disagree with each other — most sharply on the tone sandhi table, where sources contradict
one another outright — and this dataset has not been checked by a native speaker. The tone
assignments in particular are provisional.

A dataset that presents [tie⁵⁵] for 潮 with the same visual authority as a well-attested mapping
is making a claim it cannot support. But suppressing the uncertain mappings would leave the
dictionary largely empty, and tracking uncertainty only in prose (a `REVIEW.md` section a consumer
never reads) does not reach anyone consuming `dist/dict.json`.

There is a second, related problem: a `confidence: high` with no evidence behind it is an
assertion. Raising a confidence should be an argument someone can check.

## Decision

We will attach **`confidence: high | medium | low` to every phoneme mapping**, and let a mapping
optionally cite **`sources:`** resolved against [data/sources.yaml](../../data/sources.yaml)
exactly as an entry's are.

```yaml
e:
  ipa: ɯ
  confidence: high
  sources: [pengim-1960, wikipedia, learnteochew]
```

A derived form propagates the **weakest** mapping it used: `ipa_confidence` and `ipa_caveats` ship
on every reading in `dist/dict.json`, so a consumer sees the uncertainty without re-deriving it.
Unsettled areas are additionally flagged in the data itself — the sandhi table
([data/phonology/sandhi/chaozhou.yaml](../../data/phonology/sandhi/chaozhou.yaml)) carries
`needs_review: true` in full — and tracked as numbered open questions in
[data/phonology/REVIEW.md](../../data/phonology/REVIEW.md), each listing the test words that would
resolve it.

The validator enforces mapping provenance rather than treating `sources:` as decoration (issue #12,
2026-07-31), and a mapping's `sources:` may cite either an `import` or a `reference` source, unlike
an entry's (ADR-0009) — a mapping records evidence about the language, not the origin of copied
content.

## Consequences

- **Uncertainty is data, not prose.** A downstream consumer can filter to high-confidence
  readings, and the web UI can present a caveat, without anyone re-reading `REVIEW.md`.
- **Raising a confidence is reviewable.** The `sources:` field turns "we're now sure about `ê`"
  into a claim with citations attached — which is exactly how § 1's `e`/`ê` split was resolved
  (issue #2, 2026-07-27) and how § 3's sandhi contours were corrected (issue #7, 2026-07-30).
- Publishing an incomplete, honestly-labelled analysis is possible. The project ships now and
  improves, rather than waiting on a native-speaker pass it does not yet have.
- Negative: contributors editing phonology must supply a confidence, and are asked for sources
  they may not have. The `medium`/`low` tiers exist so that "I'm not sure" is a legal answer
  rather than a reason to skip the entry.
- Negative: weakest-link propagation is coarse — a reading with one low-confidence mapping and
  five high ones reports `low`. That is the conservative direction, and deliberate.
- `REVIEW.md` sections are resolved in place, marked "✅ Resolved <date>", rather than deleted
  (issue #2), so the history of what was once uncertain survives.

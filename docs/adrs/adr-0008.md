# ADR-0008: Derive an Entry's Licence and Attributions from Its Sources

## Status

✅ Accepted

## Context

ADR-0007 makes the data licence non-uniform: an entry is CC-BY-4.0 by default, CC-BY-SA-4.0 if it
draws on Wiktionary or CC-CEDICT, and may owe a separate notice to Unicode for `unihan`.

Recording that as a hand-written `licence:` field on each entry has the drift problem of every
hand-maintained derived field (ADR-0001): the moment someone adds a Wiktionary gloss to an entry
without also editing its `licence:`, the dataset is misdescribing its own terms — which is a legal
statement, not a cosmetic one.

Leaving it uncomputed is worse. A consumer who wants only the permissive subset would have to
re-implement the share-alike rule themselves, from prose, correctly.

## Decision

**An entry's licence is derived from its `sources`, never hand-written.**
[src/data/licence.ts](../../src/data/licence.ts) computes it, and every entry in `dist/dict.json`
and `dist/dict.sqlite` ships two fields that answer two different questions:

- **`licence`** — what governs redistributing the entry. `BASE_LICENCE` (CC-BY-4.0) unless a cited
  source is share-alike, in which case that source's licence covers the **whole entry**. A merged
  record is an adaptation, not a mere collection, so it cannot keep its parts separately licensed.
- **`attributions`** — whose notice must *also* be retained: every cited source whose own licence
  differs from `BASE_LICENCE`, share-alike or not. A permissive-but-distinct licence never moves
  `licence` — citing `unihan` (Unicode-DFS-2016) leaves an entry CC-BY-4.0 — but its notice still
  travels with the entry rather than being silently folded in (issue #6, 2026-07-29). CC0 sources
  are classed public-domain and owe no notice.

`withProjectAttribution` adds this project's own credit (`PROJECT_ATTRIBUTION`), so an entry whose
sources are all `BASE_LICENCE` still carries an attributable name (issue #69, 2026-08-18).

Audio clips follow the same rule, resolved from the clip's own `sources` — see ADR-0015.

## Consequences

- The dataset's licence statement cannot go stale: adding a Wiktionary source to an entry
  relicenses it automatically and visibly at build time.
- **A consumer can filter to a purely permissive subset** by reading one field, without
  re-deriving the rule. That is the point of shipping it rather than documenting it.
- Splitting `licence` from `attributions` prevents the two failure modes of a single field:
  collapsing a distinct permissive licence into the base one (losing a required notice), and
  treating any non-base licence as contaminating (wrongly marking entries share-alike).
- Negative: the rule lives in code, so an external consumer reading `data/entries/*.yaml` directly
  — rather than `dist/` — must apply it themselves. Documented in the README's licensing table.
- Negative: whole-entry share-alike propagation is the conservative reading. An entry that cites
  Wiktionary only for a tag becomes CC-BY-SA-4.0 in full. Deliberate: the alternative is
  per-field licensing, which is unenforceable and unhelpful to a reuser.
- Depends on ADR-0009 for its input: the rule is only sound because a `reference` source cannot be
  cited by an entry at all.

# ADR-0011: Enumerate Wiktionary Headwords by `insource:` Search, Not a Mandarin Frequency List

## Status

✅ Accepted

## Context

ADR-0010 needs a denominator for the Wiktionary-derived side of the lexicon: which headwords does
Wiktionary actually document a Teochew reading for?

The original plan (**issue #53**) was to work from a **Mandarin frequency list** — take the most
common Mandarin words, and try each as a Teochew headword. It is easy to obtain and needs no
special tooling.

Its problem is that it is a guess about a different language. A high-frequency Mandarin word may
have no Teochew page at all; a core Teochew word may be rare or absent in Mandarin. The list
produces a work queue whose hit rate is unknown in advance and whose misses are indistinguishable
from genuine gaps: every `no_reading` is ambiguous between "Wiktionary doesn't document this" and
"this headword guess was wrong". That ambiguity is exactly what the Swadesh list's 16 remaining
`no_reading` items demonstrate — they need better headwords before they can even be re-fetched.

## Decision

We will **enumerate the set directly** instead of guessing at it.

`npm run wordlist:wiktionary` (issue #54, 2026-08-17) uses CirrusSearch's full-text
`insource:/mn-t=/` query over namespace 0 to list **every Wiktionary page whose wikitext declares a
Teochew reading**, and classifies each hit against `data/entries/`. Issue #53's frequency-list
approach is **superseded** by it.

The consequence that makes this work: because the query selects on the presence of a Teochew
reading, the page title *already is* the headword. No headword curation step exists, and every hit
is by construction a page that has something to give.

## Consequences

- **The denominator is the real set, not a proxy for it.** 15,845 headwords, enumerated rather than
  estimated — which is what made issue #68's batch-merge workflow plannable at all.
- `no_reading` becomes meaningful. Within this checklist it can only mean the importer found
  nothing on a page that declared a reading, not that someone guessed a wrong headword.
- No hand curation, so the checklist scales to five figures without a bottleneck ahead of the
  fetch — the bottleneck moves entirely to the human merge step (ADR-0006), which is where the
  irreducible cost actually is.
- Negative: coverage is now defined by *Wiktionary's* coverage. Words no one has added to
  Wiktionary are invisible to this method, so it complements rather than replaces the hand-curated
  Swadesh list.
- Negative: the approach depends on a Wikimedia search feature and on the `mn-t=` template
  convention holding. A change to either breaks enumeration — though not the already-enumerated
  checklist, which is committed.
- The scale this unlocked is what forced the caching decisions in ADR-0013: 15,845 headwords is
  too many to fetch live, twice, per merge batch.
